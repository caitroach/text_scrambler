Lapin, or bunnies, are small mammals in the family Leporidae (which also includes the hares), which is in the rules of order Lagomorpha (which too includes coney). They are familiar throughout the Earth as a small herbivore, a prey animal, a domesticated phase of livestock, and a pet, having a widespread effect on ecologies and cultures. The nearly widespread rabbit genera are Oryctolagus and Sylvilagus. The former, Oryctolagus, includes the European rabbit, Oryctolagus genus Cuniculus, which is the ancestor of the hundreds of breeds of domestic rabbit and has been introduced on every continent except Antarctica. The latter, Sylvilagus, include over 13 wild rabbit species, among them the cottontail and tapetis. Wild rabbits not included in Oryctolagus and Sylvilagus include several specie of limited distribution, including the pygmy rabbit, vent rabbit, and Sumatran striped rabbit.

Chaos theory is a offset of mathematics and an interdisciplinary area of scientific field. It focuses on underlying patterns and deterministic laws of dynamical systems that are highly sensitive to initial conditions. These were once thought to have completely random states of disorder and geometrical irregularity. The theory states that within the apparent randomness of chaotic complex systems, there are underlying patterns, interconnection, constant feedback loops, repeating, self-similarity, fractals and self-organization.The butterfly impression, an underlying principle of chaos, describes how a small modification in one state of a deterministic nonlinear system can result in large differences in a later state (meaning there is sensitive dependence on initial conditions). A metaphor for this behavior is that a butterfly flapping its wings in Brazil can cause or prevent a tornado in Texas.
